Making a Menu That Feels Like 2002

Recreating the tactile, chunky UI of early-2000s PC games required rethinking modern design patterns. We ditched smooth gradients for hard-edged bevels, added pixel-perfect borders, and embraced the chaotic charm of arcade-style navigation. The result: a menu system that feels like booting up an old shareware title, but with today's responsiveness.

CRT Bloom Without the Mess

Achieving authentic CRT bloom and scanline effects without tanking performance was a puzzle. We experimented with layered box-shadows, subtle text-shadows, and carefully tuned RGBA overlays to mimic phosphor glow. The trick was balancing nostalgia with readability—too much bloom and text becomes illegible; too little and the retro vibe disappears.

Arcade Difficulty Curves

Classic arcade games were designed to eat quarters, but modern players expect fairness. We studied the difficulty pacing of games like Galaga and Pac-Man, then adapted those principles for a single-player experience. The goal: challenge that feels tough but never cheap, with clear skill progression and satisfying mastery moments.

Reducing Input Lag Feel

Even a few milliseconds of input lag can ruin the snappy, arcade-tight feel we're chasing. We optimized frame timing, reduced render pipeline overhead, and added subtle visual feedback (like button press animations) to make controls feel instant. The difference is invisible on paper but critical in practice—players notice when movement feels sluggish.

From Prototype to Demo

Turning a rough prototype into a polished demo meant cutting features, refining core mechanics, and obsessing over the first 5 minutes of gameplay. We learned that a focused, tight experience beats a sprawling, half-baked one every time. The demo became a love letter to the arcade era—compact, replayable, and unapologetically fun.
